The platforms profit, but what's left for us? Are we just the foundation they're built on? ## The Indie Artist's Unpaid Labor

Let's break down the transaction. An independent artist invests time and money to create and distribute music. They then direct their hard-earned audience to a streaming service. Each new fan-subscriber represents recurring revenue for the platform. In return, the artist receives micro-pennies per stream, a fraction often swallowed by distributor fees. This model positions artists as unpaid customer acquisition teams and content providers, fueling a system where **activity** (our labor) builds **popularity** (their profit), with little flowing back to the source.

## Core Principles for a Fairer System

This extractive model is antithetical to the values that should sustain art. At Sonora.Chat, we built our platform on mantras that directly counter this dynamic:

*   **Zero cost to publish or be heard:** There are no fees to upload your music. Period. This eliminates the initial "pay-to-play" barrier that already puts artists at a deficit.
*   **Direct fan support, no middlemen:** When fans want to support you, they can do so via integrated links to Ko-fi or Buy Me a Coffee, with 0% commission taken by Sonora. What fans give, you keep.
*   **Bots don't matter here:** Visibility isn't gamed by fake streams or ad budgets. It's driven by genuine fan engagement, ensuring the platform rewards real human connection, not just numbers on a spreadsheet.
